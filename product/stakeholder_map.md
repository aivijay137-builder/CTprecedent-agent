# Part 1: Stakeholder map

How to read this. Every row is one job title. "Evidence" means a job posting or published source I opened in search results on 2026-11-04. "[HYPOTHESIS]" means it is my informed guess and you should test it in an interview. "Not found" means my searches did not surface it, with a suggestion for how to find it.

Plain-language glossary (first use):
- **CI (competitive intelligence)**: people who track what rival companies are doing and brief their own leaders.
- **CRO (contract research organization)**: a company a drug developer hires to run a trial for them.
- **Sponsor**: the organization that pays for and is legally responsible for a trial.
- **Feasibility**: the pre-trial exercise of deciding whether a trial can enroll enough patients, in which countries and at which sites.
- **Site**: a hospital or clinic that enrolls patients.
- **SMO (site management organization)**: a company that runs a network of sites and handles the paperwork for them.
- **RFP (request for proposal)**: a document a sponsor sends to CROs asking them to bid.
- **Bid defense**: the meeting where a CRO presents its bid to the sponsor.
- **PI (principal investigator)**: the lead doctor or scientist responsible for a trial at a site or at a university.

Two honest limits up front:
1. Job postings tell you duties and reporting lines. They almost never tell you KPIs, tool budgets or how often someone opens trial data. Those columns are mostly [HYPOTHESIS].
2. No budget figures for any role were found. Price points in the "Tools today" columns come from your earlier research and from vendor pages, and I did not re-verify the earlier-research prices.

Why this role matters: DeVito et al. (Lancet 2020) found 40.9% of 4,209 trials due to report results did so within the one-year deadline, with academic and government sponsors worst ([Lancet cohort as summarized by TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/most-clinical-trial-sponsors-fail-report-data-mandated-fda)). A secondary source reports the FDA sent reminder letters in March 2026 to over 2,200 sponsors; I did not verify that against an FDA release.

## What this map says

- The buyers with a clear daily need to watch other people's trials are CI analysts, feasibility leads, and investor analysts (evidence: postings name ClinicalTrials.gov and Trialtrove in their duties).
- The buyers whose pain is about their own trial are disclosure managers and site coordinators.
- Small biotech has no dedicated CI role in the postings I found. That makes the real small-biotech buyer a combination of CEO, head of clinical ops and BD. Test this in interviews.
